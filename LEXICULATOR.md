LEXICULATOR
lex-i-cu-la-tor  \ LEK-si-KYOO-lay-tur \  noun

ETYMOLOGY
Blend of "lexicon" (Greek lexikon, "of or for words") and
"calculator" (Latin calculare, "to reckon with pebbles").
Coined 2026.

DEFINITIONS

1. A system that performs arithmetic on language: one that
   stores words, numbers, symbols, and punctuation as numerical
   values and computes an output from them, rather than looking
   answers up in a fixed list.
   - A large language model is a lexiculator; it does not
     retrieve the next word, it calculates it.

2. Any device or process that treats meaning as a quantity,
   converting vocabulary into figures, operating on those
   figures, and converting the result back into readable text.

3. (figurative, of a person) One who reasons about words with
   mechanical precision but without felt understanding.
   - He's a lexiculator, not a poet. He can tell you the odds a
     sentence ends a certain way, but not why it aches.

DERIVED FORMS

lexiculate (verb)
   To compute an output in words.
   - The model lexiculated a reply in under a second.

lexiculation (noun)
   The act or result of such computation.

lexiculative (adjective)
   Pertaining to word-arithmetic.
   - a lexiculative approach to translation

USAGE NOTE
A calculator takes numbers and returns numbers. A dictionary
takes a word and returns a fixed definition. A lexiculator
takes words AS numbers and returns words that were never stored
anywhere. The distinguishing feature is that the output is
generated, not retrieved.
